# FAQ

<!-- widget:accordion -->

### Does the AI pick the winners?

No, and no mode does. The panel produces an advisory read per submission; a person sets the Jury Score per dimension, and the leaderboard is built from those human scores and your weights. The AI Total Score sits beside it for reference. See [Score and shortlist](./guides/score-and-shortlist.md).

### What does EvalLens actually do with a pitch deck?

It reads the deck in full, scores it against your rubric with a panel of independent AI judges, and ties every finding to the passage it came from. Your team gets a report and a ranked board; the decision stays with you.

### How long does a batch take?

A batch is evaluated in one run, not deck by deck, so weeks of reading come back as one comparable set. The pace that matters afterwards is your review meeting.

### Do we have to change how applicants submit?

No. Add entrants by hand or open a public submission page in the Entry Hub; existing intake tools keep working. See [Collect submissions](./guides/collect-submissions.md).

### Can we use our own criteria and weights?

Yes. Weights apply at the leaderboard, not inside each judge's reading, so the same evidence can be re-ranked without re-running the batch. They lock when scoring starts. See [Criteria and weights](./guides/criteria-and-weights.md).

### We run multiple tracks with different rubrics.

Tracks, criteria and weights are configured per project, and each track scores against its own rubric and weighting.

### Will our teams' submissions train your model? Who sees them?

Never trained on — contractual. Submissions are processed only for your program's evaluation, in a workspace scoped to your project, and every report and score belongs to your program. The sub-processor list is published. See [Security and privacy](./trust/security-and-privacy.md).

### Won't teams say "an AI judged us"?

Say it before they ask: every submission received a full read under identical rules, and humans made every ranking decision. What is hard to defend is entry #300 getting a tired judge at 11pm.

### Judging is how we engage sponsors and alumni. Are we cutting judges?

No. Judge count is a sponsor perk and a program KPI, so the ask changes, not the headcount. Remote screeners do a confirm-and-comment pass instead of a reading weekend.

### Can a team appeal, or see its own record?

Every submission has a record: scores per dimension, the evidence behind them, where reviewers disagreed, and the human score that decided the placement. Whether you run appeals is your policy; the record makes the conversation five minutes long.

### How do we validate this before it touches a real result?

Run it in parallel. Your screeners work a round as usual while the panel reads the same field; compare coverage, hours and where the pre-ranking agreed. Nothing counts until you decide it does.

### Does it review code repositories and live demos?

Not today. The panel reads the materials you already collect: the deck, the project description and the team's notes. Repository and live-URL review is the next build on the roadmap.

### Is it a fact-check?

No. Completeness flags missing or thin coverage, and prompt-injection safety keeps deck text from controlling the evaluation; neither proves a claim true. See [What EvalLens does not do](./trust/boundaries.md).

### What counts as a submission for billing?

One submitted deck that receives one successfully generated evaluation report. An unprocessable deck does not count, and neither does disagreeing with the output; a resubmission after a report does. See [Pricing](./pricing.md).

### Is there a free trial or a public sign-up?

No public sign-up. EvalLens runs a limited partner program, and access is set up after a call. Ask on that call what a first-run offer looks like for your program.
