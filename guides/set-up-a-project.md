# Set up a project

A project is one selection round. Setting it up is a five-step wizard, and the one decision that happens *before* the wizard opens is the mode.

## Step 0: choose the mode

The mode determines which panel reads the field, and it is chosen when you create the project.

| Mode | Panel | Rubric shape |
|---|---|---|
| Pitch Competition | Six judges (J-P1 … J-P6) | Six dimensions P1–P6, market and team weighted 0.20 each |
| Hackathon | Five reviewer roles | Execution and Demo 0.30 and Technical Depth 0.20, both weight-protected |

Pick the one that matches what you are actually selecting for. A hackathon judged on the pitch rubric will reward the team that demoed well over the team that shipped.

## Step 1: details

The event itself: name, what the program is, and the window you are working to. This is the information that appears to teams on a public submission page, so write it the way an applicant will read it.

## Step 2: criteria and weights

The default pitch rubric arrives with six weighted dimensions and anchor descriptions per band. Two things to decide:

- **Do the default weights match your program?** Market attractiveness and Team / founder fit carry 0.20 each; the other four carry 0.15. A demo-day cohort that has had equal coaching on story often shifts weight toward Team (P5) and Feasibility (P6) and away from pitch polish.
- **When do they lock?** Weights are editable right up until the run starts, then they freeze, so the whole field is ranked on one standard end to end.

Details and the anchor table: [Criteria and weights](./criteria-and-weights.md).

## Step 3: judges

Configure the panel for this project. In the current release, the judge lenses and their routing — which judge influences which dimension, and how much — are the fixed method of the mode you chose; what you control at project level is the criteria and their weights. See [The six judges](../panel/judges.md) for the routing matrix.

## Step 4: how decks are collected

Two ways to fill the batch, and you can use both in the same project:

- **Add teams yourself.** From the project page: name, project, pitch deck, optional note for the jury.
- **Open a submission page.** Publish a public page, share one link, and teams upload their own decks after signing in with Google.

Access rules, the submission window, invites and sharing are covered in [Collect submissions](./collect-submissions.md).

## Step 5: final review

The last screen is the one worth slowing down on, because two things become hard to change afterwards: the weights lock when scoring starts, and a published submission page has already been shared.

A short pre-flight:

- Do the weights reflect what your jury will actually argue about?
- Does the submission page say what teams must send, and by when?
- Does your program's rules document have a methodology statement in it — what the AI panel assists with, what the judges decide, and how a team can ask about its own record?
- Do your judges know they will receive a briefing rather than a blank scorecard?

## What happens after setup

The project appears on your dashboard with its status. Nothing is evaluated until you launch a run, and a run needs at least one entry marked ready.
