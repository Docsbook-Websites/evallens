# Accelerators

**Accelerator management software** is how a program tracks applications, coordinates reviewers and runs cohort selection — application platforms like F6S, YouNoodle or Submittable handle intake and logistics. EvalLens is the review layer on top of that: every application read in full with an evidence-linked score, so your committee decides from verified findings instead of raw submissions.

A cohort intake is a volume problem with a reputation attached: hundreds of applications in a two-to-three-week window, a team of two to five, and a rejection that most founders never get a reason for. This page is how that round runs with a structured first read underneath it.

## Intake in numbers

| Figure | What it is |
|---|---|
| 150–2,000 | Applications per cohort, from a regional program's team of two to a global intake's team of five |
| 2–3 min | The read a founder's six months of work gets under deadline pressure |
| #447 ≠ #1 | Late applications get a different read than early ones — recency and fatigue |
| 0 | Substantive feedback most rejected founders receive |

## Five steps to run an accelerator intake

<!-- widget:stepper -->

### Keep your intake

Founders keep applying through F6S, Typeform, YouNoodle or your own form; the batch is ingested — structured answers, decks, video links. Batch export (CSV plus files) works today; platform connectors are scoped during setup. Or use a branded application page instead. Nothing changes for founders.

### Full read of every application, every page

Form answers and decks are read in full with **coverage logged**, so you can verify what was read. Application #1 and #1,400 get the same rules on the same day quality.

### Independent reviewer roles score on your rubric

Independent AI reads — team, market, feasibility, traction — that cannot anchor on each other: each scores without seeing the others' numbers. Evidence comes before the score, and incomplete evidence moves scores down, never up. Where reads split by more than a band, the case escalates to your team as an open question instead of being averaged.

### Committee decides from evidence-linked reports

Your mentors, partners and screeners keep scoring and arguing exactly as they do — starting from evidence-linked reports instead of raw applications. Confirmations and overrides are logged; the shortlist is built from human decisions. Selection Day time goes to the borderline cases.

### Feedback and the record

Feedback is drafted from the evidence and approved by your team before anything is sent, so every rejected founder gets a real answer under your name. The program keeps a selection memo and full decision log — the audit trail LP-funded and publicly funded programs are asked for.

## Retro-test on decisions you already made

Test it against decisions you already made; your last cohort is ground truth you own.

<!-- widget:stepper -->

### Day 0: share a closed batch

20–50 applications from a previous cohort, NDA first if you prefer.

### 48 hours: reports and a ranked board

Evidence-linked reports and a ranked board, on your own rubric.

### Comparison call: side by side

Your committee's actual decisions against the panel's: where it agreed, where it diverged, and why, with page references.

## Score breakdown: what one number unpacks into

A worked example on Team readiness:

- **Score** — 7.8, confidence high.
- **Finding** — *two founders shipped a comparable product at their previous company.*
- **Quote** — *"…led the payments team from prototype to 200k users…"* · page 11.
- **Interview question** — *"Walk us through what you shipped there."*

Every quote is verified against the source document: no quote, no finding. When a partner asks why you passed on someone, the answer has a page number.

## Day 0 versus Selection Day

**You bring** your criteria and weights, your existing intake form and this cohort's batch. If the rubric lives in your committee's heads, the setup session writes it down, and it is yours to keep either way.

**You hold on Selection Day:**

- every application fully read, with a coverage log;
- an evidence-linked report and interview questions per team;
- a ranked board with the borderline flagged;
- a shortlist built from your committee's scores;
- approved feedback for every founder;
- a selection memo and decision log for the board.
